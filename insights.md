# Insights

## Consistency and detraining
- 2026-09-28. Consistency is the binding constraint, not fitness. Aug to Sep 2026 averaged 4.7 real hours a week with 30% of runs and 14% of swims completed, and every fitness marker moved with the gaps. High. (reports/2026-year-review.md)
- 2026-09-28. Fitness follows consistency with a four to six week lag: best markers in Jan 2026 after the Oct to Jan block, worst in April after a 38-day stop, July back to January's run numbers, Aug to Sep slipping after two 7 to 11 day gaps. Medium.
- 2026-09-28. The 38-day stop after the Feb 70.3 cost about 20% of run pace at a fixed heart rate and about 45% of bike power at a fixed heart rate. The run took 12 to 14 interrupted weeks to come back; the bike had not by September (heat is part of that). Medium.
- 2026-09-28. Shorter gaps cost less but stack: each 7 to 11 day gap pushed bike HR at 106 W up 15 to 20 bpm and run pace at HR 165 out by 30 to 45 s/km, with a return to near-form within about three weeks. Medium.
- 2026-09-28. Skips cluster by day, not by session length: Monday runs 23% done, Sunday swims 4%, Friday swims 32%, Saturday bikes 41%; Tuesday to Thursday 60 to 70%. Long sessions are completed as often as short ones. High.
- 2026-09-28. Low weeks follow low weeks, not spikes: the week before a sub-40% week averaged 51% completion and 30% less load than usual. Only one gap (April) followed a load jump. High on the pattern, medium on the cause.
- 2026-09-28. The best fitness block (Dec 2025 to Feb 2026) was 55% easy, 41% moderate, 5% hard at 8 to 10 h/week. The 2026 Bangalore months doubled the hard share while cutting hours by a third and produced the worst markers. Medium (fixed HR bands across sports).

## Heart rate, thresholds and pacing
- 2026-09-28. Run threshold is 168 to 172, not Garmin's 178: heart rate flat at 163 to 167 for 70 min (27 Sep), runaway above 168 to 170 on the treadmill (20 Sep), bike threshold reps at 163 to 167 plus the usual 5 to 8 bpm run offset. High.
- 2026-09-28. 165 bpm is sustainable for a full half marathon: 70 min at 164 to 167, 2% drift, negative split (27 Sep). High.
- 2026-09-28. Stepped heart-rate caps work for Rajat: executed within 2 bpm for 21 km with the fastest kilometre last. The method is in races/pacing-model.md. High.
- 2026-09-28. Time above threshold roughly halves per 5 bpm (about 25 min at 175, 12 at 180, 5 at 185 when fresh). Low to medium; wrist data at the top end.
- 2026-09-28. Wrist HR is not usable for cap-based pacing (jumps of 18 bpm in 20 s, one rep 25 bpm low), although the 27 Sep trace was clean. Strap or optical arm band needed for Goa. High.
- 2026-09-28. Wrist maxima of 197 and 199 (19 Aug, 20 Sep) rose gradually and sat above 185 for minutes, so a true max near 200 stands. Medium.

## Heat, drift and environment
- 2026-09-28. Drift (heart rate rising at constant effort) is large indoors and modest outdoors: 9 to 21% on indoor rides without a fan, 2 to 5% on outdoor runs. Fan for quality sessions; heat exposure only when planned. Medium-high.
- 2026-09-28. The bike drift is partly Rajat's physiology, not only the room: 20% in the Gurgaon air-conditioned room in Oct 2025, 10% in Jan, 11 to 18% in Bangalore. Some heat adaptation over the summer (18.5% in April to 10.6% in September). Low-medium.
- 2026-09-28. Hydration and salt change the second half: 23 Sep (under-hydrated, evening heat) 5% drift and exhaustion at minute 80; 27 Sep (water every station, salt schedule) 2% drift and a negative split. Medium (conditions differed too).
- 2026-09-28. Cooler air is worth 10 to 15 s/km at the same heart rate (28 C vs 22 C). Medium.
- 2026-09-28. Off the bike Rajat runs faster, hotter and fades more: outdoor bricks average HR 153 at 6:48/km with 8% drift against HR 148 at 7:30/km with 2% for standalone runs. For Goa, hold the first 2 km off the bike 20 s/km slower than it feels. Medium, 8 bricks.

## Weight and the engine
- 2026-09-28. The engine is bigger than the running shows: resting HR mid-40s and a VO2max estimate of 46 to 50 would normally give a 2:00 to 2:05 half, not 2:21. The gap is weight (7 kg over usual), easy-pace form (cadence 155 to 162, ground contact 300 ms) and low run volume. Expect fast early gains to about 2:10 from weight alone. Medium-high.
- 2026-09-28. Most of the VO2max-estimate drop is arithmetic from the weight gain (49.6 x 76/83 = 45.4 vs 45.8 seen). Medium.
- 2026-09-28. Run form improved over the year (cadence 164 to 170, contact 282 to 274 ms at the same pace) while heart rate at that pace rose 10 to 13 bpm since July. The loss is metabolic (weight, heat, volume), not mechanical. Medium.
- 2026-09-28. A 1:45 open half is an 18 to 24 month project; sub 2:00 by Feb or Mar 2027 is likely if weight returns to 76 and run frequency reaches 4 to 5 a week. Medium.
- 2026-09-28. A 70.3 run leg is 10 to 12% slower than his open half in cool conditions, plus 5 to 8% for Goa heat. Medium, one data point.

## Recovery
- 2026-09-28. Resting HR rose from 47 to 51 (ten months) to 56.5 in Aug and 62 in Sep 2026, the largest change in any wellness series this year, and it predates the poor Aug to Sep training. Rajat attributes it to alcohol; weight and fewer nights with the watch on also contribute. Track weekly. Medium on size, low on cause.
- 2026-09-28. Sleep medians 6.4 to 7.0 h except Apr to May 2026 (5.2 to 5.6 h, late TV). HRV drifted from 43 to 48 (Sep 2025 to Jan 2026) to 38 to 41 (Jun to Sep 2026). Low-medium, sparse recent data.

## Data quality
- 2026-09-28. TrainingPeaks overstates volume by 30 to 90% in bike-heavy weeks because MyWhoosh rides upload twice; the scripts deduplicate, so any decision built on TrainingPeaks totals is wrong. High.
- 2026-09-28. TrainingPeaks run and swim load numbers (TSS) are unusable all year from misconfigured thresholds; the repo's HR load replaces them. High.
- 2026-09-28. The Sep to Oct 2025 swim pace of 2:13/100 m is probably a pool-length artefact (Gurgaon pool length unknown); the usable swim series starts in November. Low-medium.
