# Session 2026-09-29: High Power Big Gear Strength Work (MyWhoosh, indoor, 07:39 IST)

Executed as well as this kind of session can be: five of six work reps landed within 2 W of each other, so the only things to tidy are how each rep starts and how the recoveries are ridden.

## What the session was
A big gear session means riding hard at a very low pedalling rate (cadence, in revolutions per minute), so each pedal stroke carries a lot of force. It trains leg strength and the ability to push a bigger gear, not the heart and lungs, which is why heart rate stays lower than the power alone would suggest. Structure as ridden, from the lap markers in the file: 20 min ramp warm-up (55 W rising to 147 W), 5 min easy, 6 x 2 min at 215 W with 3 min recovery at 75 W, 2 min easy, 15 min steady at 138 W, 5 min cool-down. 77 min, 113 W average, 33.9 km virtual. Power from the Tacx trainer and heart rate from the chest strap paired to MyWhoosh (both high trust; the file lists no HR device, so the scripts first labelled it wrist and Rajat corrected it). Ceiling fan on, little sun until the end.

215 W is about 140% of the estimated 155 W FTP (the highest power that can be held for roughly an hour), which sits in the coach's Z5 to Z6 range for this session in earlier versions of it. Heart rate rose 43 to 51 bpm during each rep and dropped 20 to 31 bpm in the first minute of recovery, in line with the recovery figures in athlete.md. Peaks of 155 to 163 are well below the 172 to 175 seen on 2 min reps at normal cadence: at 45 to 50 rpm the limit is leg force, not oxygen delivery, so the heart never gets pushed as hard.

Rep 1 stalled at the start: cadence fell to 20 rpm and power read 123 W for the first 15 s before jumping to 226 W. Reps 4 and 6 opened with spikes of 263 and 240 W. Recoveries were ridden at 47 to 59 rpm, so the legs never got a spin between reps.

## The steady block
15 min at 138 W (about 89% of estimated FTP) at 75 rpm. Heart rate 140 in the first half, 146 in the second, a 6 bpm rise after six hard reps. Tidy.

## To improve next time
1. Drop one gear before each rep starts and build force over the first 5 s. Removes the stall in rep 1 and the 240 to 263 W spikes, which are wasted at this intensity.
2. Keep cadence at 50 or above, not 40 to 45. The work is the same; the knees carry less.
3. Spin the 3 min recoveries at 85 to 95 rpm. The contrast between grinding and spinning is part of the training; staying at 50 rpm in recovery only adds fatigue.
