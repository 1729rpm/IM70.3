# 2026 year in review: 17 Sep 2025 to 27 Sep 2026

Written 28 Sep 2026 from the full TrainingPeaks export (291 activity files, 592 TrainingPeaks rows, 13 months of Garmin daily metrics). Tables: `processed/activities.csv`, `weekly.csv`, `fitness_curves.csv`. Confidence is stated per conclusion. Rajat's RPE and feel ratings were ignored. HR is wrist on 85 of 89 runs and every swim, chest strap on 48 of 90 rides; that limit sits under every run and swim conclusion.

## 1. The year in one paragraph

Real training was 6.5 h/week in Sep to Dec 2025 at 70 to 100% completion, 4.5 h/week in Jan to Mar 2026 (a good January, then a 38-day stop after the 14 Feb 70.3), 3.9 h/week in Apr to Jun at 44% completion (move from Gurgaon to Bangalore, two more gaps), and 4.8 h/week in Jul to Sep at 41%. Only 14 of 43 planned weeks reached 80% completion. Fitness tracked consistency with roughly a four to six week lag: the best month on every curve was January 2026 (after the Oct to Jan block), the worst was April (after the 38-day gap), July recovered to January's run numbers at 3 kg more body weight, and Aug to Sep slipped again after two 7 to 11 day gaps. Weight went 78 (Jan) to 75 (Feb) to 78 (Aug) to 83 kg (Sep). The 27 Sep half (2:21:30 at HR 164) was run on 39% completion and 11.8 run hours in eight weeks; the Feb 70.3 (5:39) was built on 68% completion and 68 total hours in eight weeks.

### Conclusions

- **Lag from consistency to the curves is about four to six weeks.** January's numbers follow the Oct to Jan block; July's follow Jun to mid-Jul (49%, 46%); the Aug to Sep drop follows the low stretch from 20 Jul to 7 Sep (two gaps, 11 to 43% weeks). Medium confidence: 13 monthly points and two confounders (weight, the Bangalore heat).
- **Detraining after the 38-day gap (14 Feb to 24 Mar) was large and uneven by sport.** Run pace at HR 150 went from 6:59 (Feb) to 8:23 (late Mar): about 20% slower. Bike HR at 106 W went from 116 to 139 bpm, and power at HR 133 from 134 to 75 W: about 45% less power at the same HR. Swim pace at HR 130 barely moved (2:31 to 2:25 per 100 m), but wrist HR in water is weak evidence. Medium-high confidence on direction, medium on magnitude, because the Feb bike numbers are race-taper fresh and the April ones are the first hot Bangalore rides without AC.
- **Recovery took 12 to 14 weeks of interrupted training to get the run back and has not brought the bike back.** Run at HR 160 and 165 returned to January's values by July (6:06 and 5:58 vs 6:12 and 5:56) at about 78 kg. Bike power at HR 133 was still 110 W in September against 134 in January; part of that is heat (section 4), part is detraining, and the September number sits between them. Medium confidence.
- **Shorter gaps cost less but stack.** The 7-day gap ending 6 Aug and the 11-day gap ending 7 Sep each pushed HR at 106 W up (Aug 119 to Sep 138 across the second gap) and run pace at HR 165 out by 30 to 45 s/km (Jul 5:58 to Aug 6:41). After the 11-day gap, the 27 Sep race (6:41 at 165 in 22 C) shows a return to near-August form within three weeks. Medium confidence.
- **Weight is worth roughly 6 to 8 s/km at fixed HR per kg on this data.** Between July (about 78 kg, 6:06 at HR 160) and September (83 kg, 6:59 at HR 160 in hotter evening sessions) the pace change is too large to be weight alone; the race (6:41 at 165, cool morning) is the cleaner point and gives a smaller figure. Low-medium confidence, because heat, time of day and the gaps overlap.

### What the data cannot answer

Whether the response to consistency is faster for the bike than the run. The bike proxy is confounded by the change of training room in March, and there is no bike test or race with power.

## 3. Behaviour: what gets skipped and when

From 561 planned TrainingPeaks sessions (Sep 2025 to Sep 2026):

- **Completion by sport:** bike 62%, run 49%, swim 42%, X-train 54%. Long sessions are not skipped more than short ones (bike 61% vs 63%, run 52% vs 49%).
- **Completion by day and sport:** Monday runs 23%, Sunday swims 4%, Saturday bikes 41%, Friday swims 32%. Everything on Monday and Sunday except the bike is weak. Tuesday to Thursday sits at 60 to 70%. High confidence, large sample.
- **Time of day actually trained:** 88% of swims start after 5 PM. Rides and runs split roughly 40% before 9 AM, 25% midday, 30% after 5 PM. Evening runs in Bangalore carried the worst drift (7.5% on 7 Sep at 30 C, 5.5% on 23 Sep).
- **What precedes a skipped week (completion under 40%):** the week before averages 51% completion and 233 load units, against 59% and 338 overall. Low weeks follow low weeks; the pattern is a slide, not a crash after a spike. The only gap that followed a load spike was 17 to 28 Apr (the previous week was 2.2 times the four-week average, the first full week back in Bangalore). The 38-day gap followed the race with no spike. High confidence on the pattern, medium on the cause.

## 4. Drift, heat and acclimation

- **Indoor rides decouple 12% (median of 36 rides over 60 min); outdoor rides 12%; outdoor runs 3%; treadmill runs 2.5%.** The bike drift is not new to Bangalore: Oct 2025 rides in the Gurgaon AC room show 20% (three rides), Jan 2026 10%, Bangalore months 11 to 18%. So a large part of the bike drift is Rajat's physiology at 2 to 3 h, not only the room. Medium-high confidence.
- **Time of day:** indoor rides before 9 AM decouple 12%, midday 9%, after 1 PM 10%. No clean heat-of-day signal indoors. Outdoor runs: the three cool early starts at 27 C (22 Jul, 30 Jul, 27 Sep) decoupled 0.9 to 2%; the evening runs at 28 to 30 C decoupled 5.5 to 7.5%; the 8 AM runs at 27 to 30 C sat at 4 to 6% (19 Aug with intervals, 16 Sep). Watch temperature is only recorded from June, so the Gurgaon winter runs have no reading. Medium confidence.
- **Heat acclimation:** indoor ride decoupling by month in Bangalore went 18.5% (Apr), 13.5% (Jun), 11.3% (Jul), 12.4% (Aug), 10.6% (Sep). Bike HR at 106 W did not follow a clean line (139, 132, 134, 119, 138). The decoupling trend is consistent with partial acclimation over the summer; the HR-at-power series says any acclimation is smaller than the effect of the gaps. Low-medium confidence.
- **The hall (photo, 28 Sep):** east-facing balcony door, ceiling fan only, tiled floor. Morning sun lands directly on the trainer. For quality rides: curtain closed, a floor fan aimed at the torso, ride before 8 AM or after 6 PM. This is a recommendation, not a finding.

## 5. Intensity distribution and improvement

Time-weighted share of HR samples below 140, 140 to 160, and 160 and above, all sports:

| Period | Easy | Moderate | Hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sep to Nov 2025 | 70% | 24% | 5% |
| Dec 2025 to Feb 2026 | 55% | 41% | 5% |
| Mar to May 2026 | 52% | 36% | 11% |
| Jun to Aug 2026 | 50% | 37% | 13% |
| Sep 2026 | 39% | 37% | 24% (includes the race) |

The block that produced the best curves (Dec to Feb) was 55/41/5: lots of steady moderate work, very little above 160. The Bangalore months have doubled the hard share while cutting total hours by a third. With 4 to 5 h/week, a quarter of it above HR 160 is a high-intensity, low-volume pattern, and the curves did not reward it. Medium confidence: the zones use fixed HR cut-offs across sports, and bike HR runs 5 to 8 bpm lower than run HR at the same effort.

## 6. Brick runs vs standalone runs

Runs starting within 45 minutes of a ride ending (15 of 89 runs): outdoor bricks average HR 153 at 6:48/km with 8.4% decoupling; outdoor standalone runs HR 148 at 7:30/km with 2% decoupling. Rajat runs faster off the bike, at higher HR, and fades more. Indoor bricks (7) show HR 138 at a corrected 8:12/km, so the treadmill bricks were run genuinely easy. For Goa the outdoor brick pattern (fast start, 8% fade) is the one to reverse: hold the first 2 km off the bike 20 s/km slower than it feels. Medium confidence, small sample.

## 7. Run economy trend

Outdoor runs at 6:30 to 7:00/km, medians per month: cadence 164 and ground contact 282 ms with vertical ratio 9.4% in Jan to Feb 2026 (at 75 to 78 kg, HR 150); cadence 168 to 170, contact 274 to 277 ms, vertical ratio 9.2 to 9.3% in Jun to Sep 2026 (HR 147 to 163). Mechanics at a fixed pace have improved slightly over the year (higher cadence, shorter contact, lower vertical ratio) while HR at that pace has gone up 10 to 13 bpm since July. So the economy loss is metabolic (weight, heat, less training), not mechanical. Medium confidence; wrist HR, and Sep runs were hotter.

## 9. Swim

Pace at HR 128 to 134 per 100 m: 2:13 to 2:15 in Sep to Oct 2025 (Gurgaon pool, length unknown), 2:41 to 2:43 in Nov to Dec, 2:31 to 2:34 in Jan to Feb (Feb includes the race open-water swim at 2:23 for 1.8 km), 2:25 to 2:30 in Mar to Jun in the Bangalore 25 m pool, 2:38 in Aug. The Sep to Oct 2025 figure is probably a pool-length artefact (the watch counting lengths in a longer pool), so the usable series starts in November and is roughly flat at 2:25 to 2:40. With 67 swims in the year, 42% completion and 4% of Sunday swims done, there was never a block long enough to move the swim. Stroke count and SWOLF are in the length records and have not been extracted yet. Low-medium confidence throughout (wrist HR in water).

## 11. Data-quality findings that change how the year should be read

- Run TSS in TrainingPeaks is unusable all year (threshold pace about 4:35/km until 6 Feb, about 2:10/km after). `weekly.csv` carries an HR-based load instead. High confidence.
- 39 of 291 files are duplicate uploads (every MyWhoosh ride also recorded by the watch). TrainingPeaks weekly hours were inflated 30 to 90% in bike-heavy weeks. High confidence.
- The two wrist maxima of 197 and 199 (19 Aug, 20 Sep) rose gradually (no jump above 6 bpm per second), came at 5:04 to 5:35/km, and sat above 185 for 7 and 3.6 minutes. That is consistent with real values near a true max of about 200, not optical spikes. Medium confidence.
- Garmin resting HR medians: 47 to 51 for ten months, 56.5 in Aug 2026, 62 in Sep. Rajat attributes it to alcohol with no other change. Weight (plus 5 kg since August) and fewer nights with the watch on (16 to 19 readings a month vs 25 to 31) also contribute. Whatever the cause, a 10 to 15 bpm rise in resting HR is the single largest change in any wellness series this year and predates the poor Aug to Sep training. Worth tracking weekly. Medium confidence on the size, low on the cause.
- Sleep medians 6.4 to 7.0 h except Apr to May 2026 (5.2 to 5.6 h, late-night TV, few readings). HRV medians drifted from 43 to 48 (Sep 2025 to Jan 2026) to 38 to 41 (Jun to Sep 2026). Low-medium confidence, sparse recent data.

## 12. Ranked: the five changes that would move fitness most before Venice-Jesolo (24 Apr 2027)

1. **Make Monday, Friday and Sunday sessions survivable, or move them.** Monday runs 23%, Sunday swims 4%, Friday swims 32%. Ask the coach to put the week's two hardest sessions on Tuesday to Thursday (60 to 70% completion) and make the Monday and Sunday slots short and easy, or move the swim to a weekday morning. The single biggest lever in the data is completion, and the losses are concentrated on three days.
2. **No gap longer than 4 days between now and Venice, including the Diwali, December and wedding weeks.** Every 7-plus day gap cost 3 to 6 weeks; the 38-day one cost a quarter. Running and gym are possible in all three windows. A 30 min run every third day through them keeps the run curve where it is.
3. **Return to 76 kg by February.** Independent of training it is worth several s/km at fixed HR and it moves VO2max-per-kg back to 49 to 50. The plan in `athlete.md` (78 by mid-Dec, 76 by Feb, no deficit before Goa) stands.
4. **Rebalance intensity toward the Dec to Feb pattern.** 55% easy, 40% steady, 5% hard, at 8 to 10 h/week. The current 40/37/24 at 5 h/week produced the worst curves of the year. This is mostly the coach's call; the evidence is section 5.
5. **Fix the bike environment and get a real FTP number.** Fan on the torso, curtain closed, ride before 8 AM. Then a 20 min test in October so the Venice bike target (185 to 195 W) has a baseline; the best training 20 min this year is 166 W.

Not in the top five but cheap: chest strap or arm band on every run (85 of 89 runs are wrist), fix the TrainingPeaks run threshold, stop the double upload.

## 13. Open questions the data cannot settle

- True FTP (no test).
- Whether the bike detraining in April was mostly heat: needs one cool, fanned ride at 106 W in October for comparison with 8 Aug (HR 119).
- Cause of the resting HR rise.
- Gurgaon pool length (decides whether the Sep to Oct 2025 swim pace is real).
- Whether the 11 Jan duathlon result is on file anywhere (finish time and splits).
